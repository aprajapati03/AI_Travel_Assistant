## Overview
Walk the streets of Little India, and discover a harmonious blend of age-old tradition, cutting edge architecture and shops selling traditional garments and intricate jewellery.

## Atmosphere & Themed Experiences

### Sights and Sounds of Little India
With its bustling streets, busy markets and walls adorned with vivid murals, Little India is one of Singapore's most vibrant neighbourhoods. 
   
 It is easy to explore on foot, with new sights unfolding at every turn. Expect to see families shopping for fresh flowers and spices, shopkeepers arranging garlands and textiles, and worshippers congregating at temples.

### Home to Indian Heritage and Culture
Delve into the rich heritage of Little India, whose main thoroughfare, Serangoon Road, was one of the earliest roads in Singapore.

### Modernity Amid Tradition
Trends and tradition intertwine in Little India, home to hip cafes, trendy shops and historical temples.

### A Feast For The Senses
From flower garlands to exotic spices, the colourful district of Little India is a delight for the senses.

## What to Do

### Sri Veerakaliamman Temple
Built by Indian pioneers living in the Serangoon district in the 19th century, this gorgeous place of worship is dedicated to Kali the Hindu goddess and destroyer of evil.

### Indian Heritage Centre
Indian Heritage Centre's galleries and artefacts tell the enthralling story of Singapore's Indian community. The venue also houses a Singapore Visitor Centre where you can pick up handy information.

### Former House of Tan Teng Niah
Built in 1900, the former house of Tan Teng Niah is a 2-storey villa with rainbow-hued exteriors that make for the perfect photo opportunity.

### Tea & Tiffin: Cultural & Food Tour of Little India
Discover the tale of our country's Indian pioneers, tastes of tradition and our evolving cityscape on this vibrant and colourful tour with the masterful guides from Culture Curious.

## Where to Shop

### Mustafa Centre
No trip to Little India is complete without a visit to this massive department store, which sells everything from jewelry to groceries and electronics.

### Jothi Store and Flower Shop
Besides flower garlands and prayer items sold for religious occasions, this store is filled with a range of traditional Indian beauty products, homeware, kitchenware and other colourful souvenirs.

### Little India Arcade
A bustling area filled with an array of boutique shops and eateries. Discover henna art at Selvi's Beauty Salon, browse the goods at Gokulam Jewels and Craft, and indulge in dishes from Apolo's Food Village.

### Haniffa
Originally specialising in  saris  and textiles when it opened in 1962, this traditional merchant now sells a variety of jewellery, electronics and other goods.

## Where to Eat

### Tekka Centre
Tekka Centre is Singapore’s largest indoor ‘wet’ market, selling an incredible array of fresh produce, aromatic spices and dried goods.

### Banana Leaf Apolo
Located along Race Course Road, Banana Leaf Apolo is popular for its spread of delicious South Indian food. Be sure to sample the famous local delicacy, fish head curry on banana leaf.

### Bismillah Biryani
A six-time Michelin Bib Gourmand Award Winner, this heritage restaurant doles out aromatic portions of authentic  dum biryani,  a traditional rice dish.

### Olibier Rooftop Bar
Kick back, relax with a cold beer or craft cocktails and enjoy the view at this laid-back rooftop bar. Expect chill tunes from live DJ sets and even a roving fortune teller on occasion.

### Podi & Poriyal
Want to enjoy a slice of South India? Head over to Podi & Poriyal to check out its vegetarian fare, innovative cocktails and Indian snacks.

## Where to Stay

### Hilton Garden Inn Singapore Serangoon
Located at the heart of Little India, Hilton Garden Inn Singapore Serangoon is within walking distance from great shopping options like Mustafa Centre and Bugis Street.

### Wanderlust
Housed in a heritage 1920s-styled building, Wanderlust is a boutique hotel near Little India and Bugis train stations, with uniquely themed rooms.
