## Overview: A Migration of Festivities

Eight out of the eleven public holidays in Singapore are celebrations originating from its various religions and cultures. Singapore is known to be a colourful mix of cultures, faiths, and communities. Naturally, Singaporean festivals are a rich celebration of multicultural heritage, indulging in the sights, sounds, and flavours of diverse origins.

To understand how Singapore became such a melting pot of cultures, one must look back two centuries to when the country was established as a bustling trading hub in the region. Singapore's position on the world map put it in the middle of major trade routes. Vessels from all over the world stopped here to resupply or trade. Immigrants from China, India, and the Malay Archipelago flocked here to seek greater fortunes.

After long, treacherous journeys by sea, many forefathers built places of worship along the coast at Telok Ayer Street. Temples, mosques, churches, and shrines were established for immigrants to give thanks to their gods for safe passage and to ask for blessings in their new lives. Today, Telok Ayer Street has the highest concentration of national monuments in Singapore. With freedom to practise faiths freely, immigrants brought festivals celebrated by Muslims, Hindus, and Chinese communities that have grown into vibrant spectacles of modern Singapore.

## Pongal

- **Timing**: Usually in January
- **Place of Origin**: South India
- **Religious / Cultural Roots**: Tamil Harvest Festival

Pongal is a festival offering thanks to nature. Traditionally a harvest festival from South India, it is celebrated at the beginning of *Thai*, the tenth Tamil month. Cooked rice is offered to deities to give thanks for a bountiful harvest.

In Singapore, the **Pongal Bazaar at Campbell Lane** in Little India is where the local Indian community finds festival goodies, decorative items, and religious offerings, including Indian sugar cane, ginger and turmeric plants, and devotional milk pots (*palkudam*).

## Lunar New Year (Chinese New Year)

- **Timing**: Usually in January or February
- **Place of Origin**: China
- **Duration**: 15 days

Lunar New Year is the most anticipated festival among the local Chinese community. Celebrated on the first day of the Chinese Lunar Calendar, the celebration lasts for 15 days.

### Celebrations and Traditions
- **Chinatown Night Market & Light-Up**: In the month leading up to the festival, Chinatown streets are covered in red and gold decorations, massive lantern displays, street vendors touting traditional snacks, and long queues outside popular *bakwa* (barbecued pork) shops.
- **Reunion Dinner**: On New Year's Eve, families gather for a reunion dinner and watch the annual countdown broadcast from Chinatown.
- **New Year Visiting & Snacks**: People wear new clothes to welcome new beginnings, exchange blessings and red packets (*ang bao*), and eat festive treats like pineapple tarts.
- **Yusheng / Lo Hei**: The traditional raw fish salad toss ceremony where auspicious phrases are recited to usher in prosperity.
- **Kwan Im Thong Hood Cho Temple**: Packed with devotees offering incense on Chinese New Year's Eve, believing that offering the first incense brings bountiful blessings for the year.
- **River Hongbao**: A week-long extravaganza held along the Singapore River / Marina Bay since 1987, featuring giant lanterns, cultural shows, and fireworks.
- **Chingay Parade**: An annual street procession held since 1973, involving vibrant floats and performances from all ethnic communities.

## Thaipusam

- **Timing**: Usually in January or February
- **Place of Origin**: India
- **Religious Roots**: Hinduism

Thaipusam commemorates the victory of Lord Subramaniam (Lord Murugan) over evil forces with a celestial spear (*vel*). It is one of the most powerful displays of faith and devotion in Singapore.

### Traditions and Procession
- **The Kavadi**: Pious devotees carry a *kavadi*, a steel or wooden structure weighing at least 20 kg, adorned with peacock feathers, flowers, and deity images. The kavadi is secured to the devotee's body using hooks, needles, and spikes pierced through the chest, back, and face.
- **Foot Procession Route**: Devotees walk barefoot for 3 to 4 km starting from **Sri Srinivasa Perumal Temple** on Serangoon Road and finishing at **Sri Thendayuthapani Temple** on Tank Road.
- **Community Support**: Family and friends accompany the kavadi bearers, singing hymns and carrying *palkudam* (pots of milk) as offerings.

## Vesak Day

- **Timing**: Usually in May (Full-moon day)
- **Place of Origin**: Nepal / Sri Lanka
- **Religious Roots**: Buddhism

Vesak Day commemorates the birth, enlightenment, and nirvana (passing) of Gautama Buddha. Buddhists come together to renew resolutions of leading lives of morality, humility, and simplicity.

### Practices and Observance
- **Temple Visits**: Devotees visit Buddhist temples across Singapore to offer flowers, light candles, burn joss sticks, and listen to monks deliver sermons on peace and compassion.
- **Three Steps, One Bow Ritual**: Pious devotees perform the "Three Steps, One Bow" prostration ritual on the eve of Vesak Day, notably at **Kong Meng San Phor Kark See Monastery**, to repent and seek spiritual purification.

## Hari Raya Puasa (Hari Raya Aidilfitri)

- **Timing**: First day of Syawal (10th month of Islamic calendar)
- **Place of Origin**: Middle East
- **Religious Roots**: Islam

Hari Raya Puasa marks the end of Ramadan, the holy month of fasting and prayer. It is an occasion of joy, forgiveness, and community bonding.

### Traditions and Festive Food
- **Forgiveness and Unity**: Younger family members visit elders, kissing their hands to ask for forgiveness for past transgressions. Families frequently wear matching colored traditional outfits (*baju kurung*) when visiting relatives.
- **Geylang Serai Light-Up & Ramadan Bazaar**: Miles of bright light displays and hundreds of stalls selling Malay food, traditional garments, and home decor along Geylang Serai and Bussorah Street near Sultan Mosque.
- **Signature Foods**: Families feast on beef *rendang* (slow-cooked spicy coconut beef stew), *ketupat* (rice cakes woven in coconut palm leaves), *lontong* (rice cakes served in vegetable coconut curry gravy), and various traditional *kueh* (sweet cakes and pastries).

## Hari Raya Haji (Eid al-Adha)

- **Timing**: 10th day of Zulhijjah (Islamic calendar)
- **Place of Origin**: Middle East
- **Religious Roots**: Islam

Hari Raya Haji is a sacred festival marking the completion of the annual *Haj* pilgrimage to Mecca. It honours Prophet Ibrahim's willingness to sacrifice his son in obedience to God.

### Rituals
- **Korban Ritual**: At mosques across Singapore (such as the historic **Sultan Mosque** in Kampong Glam), livestock (sheep and lambs) are sacrificed following morning prayers.
- **Charity and Sharing**: The meat is partitioned and distributed to the poor, needy, and community members, exemplifying selfless charity and gratitude.

## Mid-Autumn Festival (Lantern Festival)

- **Timing**: Usually in September or October (15th day of the 8th Chinese lunar month)
- **Place of Origin**: China
- **Cultural Roots**: Chinese Lunar Tradition

Mid-Autumn Festival is celebrated during the full moon of the eighth lunar month, traditionally marking the autumn harvest.

### Customs and Delicacies
- **Lantern Displays**: Chinatown hosts dazzling lantern displays throughout the month. Children carry colorful lanterns in evening neighborhood parades.
- **Mooncakes**: Families gather to admire the full moon and share mooncakes, traditional round pastries with lotus seed or red bean paste and salted egg yolks. Modern bakeries offer creative variations including snowskin mooncakes, durian, strawberry, and truffle fillings.

## Deepavali (Diwali)

- **Timing**: Usually in October or November
- **Place of Origin**: India
- **Religious Roots**: Hinduism

Deepavali, the Festival of Lights, celebrates the triumph of light over darkness and good over evil, marking Lord Krishna's victory over the demon king Narakasura.

### Festivities in Little India
- **Street Light-Up & Bazaars**: Little India is illuminated with miles of archways, light displays, and bustling bazaars selling traditional sweets, embroidered *saris*, jewelry, and festive decorations.
- **Temple Prayers**: Devotees visit Hindu temples to offer prayers.
- **Theemithi (Fire-Walking Ceremony)**: Held a week prior to Deepavali at **Sri Mariamman Temple** (Singapore's oldest Hindu temple, founded in 1827), where devotees walk across a pit of burning wood charcoal as a vow to goddess Sri Drowpathai Amman.

## Christmas

- **Timing**: 25 December
- **Place of Origin**: Rome
- **Religious Roots**: Christianity

Christmas is celebrated both as a religious holiday honoring the birth of Jesus Christ and as a popular festive shopping season.

### Celebrations
- **Orchard Road Light-Up**: The Orchard Road shopping precinct is transformed into a festive winter wonderland with grand thematic lighting displays, street decorations, and giant Christmas trees outside major shopping centres like Centrepoint.
- **Religious Observance**: Churches hold candlelight services and midnight masses across Singapore.
- **Local Culinary Twists**: Christmas feasts often incorporate local flavors and spices, such as turkey or roast meats infused with ginger, chilli, or Asian curry seasonings.
